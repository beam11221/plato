## Reading this

The xavier tier is the whole problem: its median `R_i/T̂_i` is only 1.15 —
barely enough time to finish training before leaving range even under ideal
conditions — and 43% of its in-coverage windows are already infeasible on
arrival. Since xavier is 50% of the fleet by count, it drags the *pool-wide*
median feasible size down to 7, under `2×C=8` at C=4 and far under `2×C=16`
at C=8. This is consistent with, and considerably sharper than, the *waste
rate* the existing (non-gated, unfiltered) sumo_close_loop runs already
measured for xavier under `anchor_sleep=15` (35.7–43.5%, `README.md`/
`STATUS.md`) — waste and infeasibility-on-arrival are related but not
identical quantities (waste also counts vehicles that entered feasible and
became infeasible mid-training), and this Step A number sits right in the
same range, which is a good cross-check that both computations are looking
at the same underlying phenomenon.

At C=4 the gate is failed by a hair (median 7 vs. threshold 8) but the
*aggregate* condition ("more likely infeasible than not," 52% of timesteps
below threshold) says the same thing more strongly: for roughly half the
run, fewer than `2C` vehicles are simultaneously both in range and capable of
finishing before they leave. Per this task's own instruction, **selection is
close to forced** for a large fraction of the run — an arm cannot show its
selector's judgment when the candidate pool barely exceeds the slots being
filled.

## Addendum — median=7 did not model occupancy; the "feasible AND idle" pool is smaller

The median=7 figure above counts every in-coverage vehicle with
`R_i ≥ T̂_i` at each 1 s timestep, full stop — it has **no concept of a
vehicle being mid-dispatch**. Step A was scoped as "no FL training," so
nothing in that number excludes a vehicle that would actually be occupying
one of the `C` training slots at that instant; it silently assumes every
vehicle is always idle. It is therefore an upper bound on the pool a real
selector would see, not the pool itself.

Refined here with a lightweight deterministic occupancy simulation: a
`C`-slot closed queue over the same trace, dispatch duration = tier-mean
`T̂_i` (same table as above, not the jittered Stage 3 fleet), longest-idle-
first fairness, and — matching the real harness, Stage 0 filter OFF — a slot
is filled by any idle in-coverage vehicle **regardless of feasibility**,
since that is what will actually be dispatched once these runs execute (an
infeasible dispatch still occupies its slot and shows up as waste later, it
just doesn't block on feasibility at the dispatch decision). Feasibility is
only evaluated when reporting the "feasible AND idle" pool below — it does
not gate the occupancy model itself.

"Per round" here means every decision instant: every second at which at
least one slot is open (a slot just freed and a selector must act) —
continuous per-second sampling regardless of whether a decision is being
made would dilute the distribution with instants nobody is choosing anything.

| C | decision instants | feasible-AND-idle pool: median / p10 / p90 | fraction `< 2C` |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | 15,224 | 6.0 / 3.0 / 9.0 | 0.7771 |
| 8 | 29,224 | 4.0 / 2.0 / 7.0 | 1.0000 |

Once occupancy is accounted for, the gate fails by more, not less: median
drops from 7 (no-occupancy upper bound) to **6** at C=4, and the fraction of
decision instants below `2C` rises from 0.52 to **0.78**. The original
median=7 was already below threshold; this confirms it wasn't an artifact of
ignoring occupancy — the true pool is smaller still. The stop decision above
stands, more strongly.

## Method — why the time axis is rebuilt rather than read

PLATO's `elapsed_time` carries `report.processing_time`, a `perf_counter`
delta measured on the host (`stage2_evidence/clock_reconstruction.md`). These
eight runs shared one GPU, so that term is both large and **unequal between
arms**: the logged per-report duration exceeds the client's own `sleep_i` by
739–855 s over a run. Comparing arms on PLATO's clock would be comparing host
contention. Step D asks for `sim_time_reconstructed`, and
`analyze_stage4_task4.py` rebuilds it with Stage 2's C-slot queue replay —
dispatch *order* from the log, durations from the fleet table, nothing
host-measured on the time axis. The rebuilt clock sits 2.2–2.9 % below PLATO's
in every run, all of it in the expected direction.

Validation, all eight runs:

- every dispatch has exactly one report; no report is unmatched
- `uploaded == raw_in_range` on every report under `coverage_gated`; `uploaded`
  is unconditionally 1 under `always_accept` — the policies behaved as declared
- the run's own `raw_in_range` is reproduced from `coverage_n20r400.csv` on
  **34,770 of 34,773** reports. The three misses are all a vehicle finishing
  exactly on a window's closing second, where the log's `%.2f` rounding pulls a
  finish that was a hair outside back onto the boundary, and windows are closed
  at both ends. All metrics use the log's `raw_in_range`, which is what the run
  acted on; the recomputation is only the cross-check.
- PLATO's own aggregation order is ascending in its logged finish time with
  **0** inversions. Against the *rebuilt* clock there are 309–425 inversions,
  median 0.31 s, max 2.1 s — near-ties reordered by host overhead, exactly the
  case Stage 2's `verify_merge_order` docstring anticipated. Sub-second jitter
  on a 6,400–9,800 s axis; it does not accumulate.
- the server's own staleness per aggregation matches `merge_round −
  dispatch_round` rebuilt from the dispatch sequence on **all 31,999**
  aggregations, 0 disagreements.

One parsing trap, recorded because it silently produces plausible numbers: the
aggregation event is `FedAsync: step N - client #C, effective_round=R`, **not**
`Adding client #N to the list of clients for aggregation`. The latter is
PLATO's pre-filter bookkeeping and fires for updates the `UploadGate` then
discards — 4,606 of them against 4,000 real aggregations in
`baseline/coverage_gated`.

## Why gating *accelerates* the decay rather than arresting it

Prediction 2 assumed avoided waste would be an advantage a good predictor could
bank. It is not, because no arm avoids waste (Step D.1) — and gating adds a
cost that falls hardest on exactly the arms the prediction expected to benefit.

Windowed xavier share, the tier that misses its window:

| policy | arm | 1–250 | 250–500 | 500–1000 | 1000–2000 | 2000–4000 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| coverage_gated | baseline | 40.5 | 38.8 | 37.8 | 37.8 | 39.8 |
| coverage_gated | gpu | 30.9 | 33.2 | 38.4 | 42.2 | **43.8** |
| coverage_gated | observed | 33.1 | 36.7 | 39.8 | 43.4 | **45.9** |
| always_accept | gpu | 33.2 | 32.0 | 39.0 | 39.9 | 42.9 |

Every scoring arm drifts from fast-tier-heavy toward — and past — `baseline`'s
share, which is the Stage 3 / Task 3 decay. Under gating that drift is taxed
twice: a xavier dispatch is both slow *and* 29 % likely to produce nothing, and
the wasted cycle has to be repeated. So the arms that end up most xavier-heavy
pay the most, and they are the scoring arms. `baseline`, which never had a
speed signal to decay from, keeps a flat share and a flat waste rate and simply
does not have the transient to lose.

Restated as the law: `T = rounds × mean_sleep(mix) / (C × (1 − waste))`, and
`waste` is a function of `mix`, so **both terms are governed by the same
quantity**. Gating did not add a new degree of freedom for a selector to
exploit; it added a second penalty on the one that already existed.

Measured against that formula:

| run | measured T | predicted T | error |
|---|---|---|---|
| baseline / coverage_gated | 7,525 s | 7,549 s | +0.3 % |
| gpu / coverage_gated | 7,992 s | 8,031 s | +0.5 % |
| cpu / coverage_gated | 9,759 s | 9,827 s | +0.7 % |
| observed / coverage_gated | 8,169 s | 8,244 s | +0.9 % |
| baseline / always_accept | 6,441 s | 6,489 s | +0.8 % |
| gpu / always_accept | 6,688 s | 6,727 s | +0.6 % |
| cpu / always_accept | 7,948 s | 7,998 s | +0.6 % |
| observed / always_accept | 6,685 s | 6,742 s | +0.9 % |

Stage 3 predicted total time from mix alone to within 0.1–1.3 points. The gated
form holds to within 0.9.

## A hypothesis this cell was asked to watch, and which it refutes

`STATUS.md` §5.4 and `HANDOFF.md` §7 flagged a feedback loop: a vehicle whose
uploads keep failing is never marked explored, keeps its 10× `G_i` proxy, and
so keeps being picked. If that bit, the gap before a wasted vehicle is
re-dispatched would be *shorter* than after a useful one, and shorter in the
scoring arms than in `baseline`.

Mean dispatch cycles until the same client is dispatched again:

| policy | arm | after waste | after useful | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| coverage_gated | baseline | 30.6 | 18.3 | 1.67 |
| coverage_gated | gpu | 30.4 | 18.2 | 1.67 |
| coverage_gated | cpu | 25.0 | 18.9 | 1.32 |
| coverage_gated | observed | 28.5 | 18.6 | 1.53 |
| always_accept | gpu | 30.3 | 18.3 | 1.65 |

**The opposite of the loop, and identical in `gpu` and `baseline`.** A wasted
vehicle waits *longer*, because being out of range at finish means it is still
out of range afterwards and the selection gate cannot offer it. The exploration
bonus does not get a chance to act on it. This does not clear the exploration
prior generally — Task 2 found `G_i`'s influence grows through a run — but the
specific waste-driven loop that was flagged as a risk for this cell is not
present in these eight runs.

## What remains unknown, and what would change this

1. **One seed (4126).** Everything above is a single draw. The *direction* of
   the Prediction 2 result rests on four quantities that agree with each other
   (crossover round, final gap, useful-work decay, mix decomposition), but the
   crossover rounds in particular are point estimates and 1,577 vs 2,477 has no
   error bar on it. Three seeds on the `coverage_gated` cell would settle it.
2. **What would overturn the conclusion**: `gpu` ending *ahead* of `baseline`
   at round 4,000 under gating on a majority of seeds. What would *not*
   overturn it: the crossover moving by a few hundred rounds, or the final gap
   moving a point or two — the claim is that the advantage is a transient, not
   that it dies at a particular round.
3. **C=8 is untested here** and Step A's gate still fails at C=8 on this trace
   (median feasible-and-idle pool 7 against `2C = 16`). Nothing in this cell
   speaks to it.
4. **Accuracy separates nothing**, as in every earlier cell: plateaus span
   0.8268–0.8351 across all eight runs, inside the ±0.05 noise band. TTA at the
   usable 75 % threshold still favours `gpu` (−21.5 % gated, −14.3 % ungated)
   **because that threshold is crossed at round 340–390, deep inside the
   transient**. TTA at an early threshold and cumulative time at round 4,000
   are measuring opposite ends of the same decay; a write-up that quotes only
   the first would describe an advantage the same runs show expiring.
