# SUMO closed-loop results — n100

Four client-selection arms under RSU coverage, against the same four arms
standing still (`../surgical/`). Generated by `analyze.py`; every number
below is read from the run logs and results CSVs, nothing is hand-entered.

**Arms finished: 4/4** (baseline, gpu, cpu, observed)

## Headline

1. **Mobility costs 20-24% of all compute the fleet performs.** A vehicle that drives out of RSU range
   before it finishes has nowhere to upload to, and that work is gone.
   The slow tier bears almost all of it: xavier wastes **38% of its dispatches**, orin and thor under 7%.
2. **The waste rate per tier is the same in all four arms** — no
   selector avoids it, because none of them knows where the vehicles
   are. What differs is only how often each arm *asks* the slow tier:
   `cpu` sends the most work to xavier and therefore wastes the
   most (24.0%), `baseline` the least (19.7%).
3. **That is the opening for the next step.** Location prediction has a
   large, well-characterised target to reduce, and this report is the
   baseline it has to beat.
4. **On accuracy, mobility is close to neutral per round and modestly
   costly per unit time** — see §2-4. Most of the individual time ratios
   sit inside the ±10-15% noise floor, so read them as a direction, not
   as measurements.

## 1. Waste computing

A vehicle that finishes training outside RSU range cannot upload. Its work
is discarded and the round it would have produced does not happen. **The
no-mobility control has none of this by construction** — that is the whole
difference between the two experiments.

| arm | rounds | dispatch cycles | wasted | waste % | xavier waste | orin waste | thor waste |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| baseline | 10,000 | 12,452 | 2,453 | **19.7%** | 37.6% | 5.2% | 2.4% |
| gpu | 10,000 | 12,495 | 2,498 | **20.0%** | 38.9% | 5.4% | 3.4% |
| cpu | 10,000 | 13,156 | 3,157 | **24.0%** | 37.9% | 5.4% | 2.8% |
| observed | 10,000 | 12,501 | 2,502 | **20.0%** | 37.9% | 5.0% | 3.6% |

Predicted from the coverage trace alone, for dispatch uniform inside a
window: xavier 43.5%, orin 5.6%, thor 2.9% (n20) / 39.5, 5.1, 2.6 (n100).

### What mobility changed about *who* gets asked

Dispatch share with the vehicles standing still, versus the same arm
under RSU coverage. This is the mechanism behind everything below: if
coverage changes the mix of clients that contribute, it changes what the
global model learns per round, independently of any timing effect.

| arm | dispatches still → moving | xavier share | xavier count Δ | orin share | orin count Δ | thor share | thor count Δ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| baseline | 10,003 → 12,455 (+2,452) | 49.6% → 46.2% | +791 | 35.9% → 37.1% | +1,032 | 14.5% → 16.7% | +629 |
| gpu | 10,003 → 12,498 (+2,495) | 45.0% → 44.6% | +1,075 | 38.3% → 38.1% | +927 | 16.6% → 17.3% | +493 |
| cpu | 10,003 → 13,159 (+3,156) | 59.7% → 58.2% | +1,689 | 28.9% → 29.4% | +973 | 11.4% → 12.4% | +494 |
| observed | 10,003 → 12,504 (+2,501) | 46.2% → 46.3% | +1,162 | 38.5% → 38.0% | +908 | 15.3% → 15.7% | +431 |

**Read the counts, not only the shares.** Mobility *adds* dispatches —
every wasted upload frees a slot that is immediately refilled — and the
fast tiers absorb the large majority of those extra cycles. So xavier's
share falls in every arm mainly because the denominator grew, not
because the selector turned against xavier: in the three predictor arms
xavier's absolute count *rises*. `baseline` is the exception, losing a
little xavier work in absolute terms as well, which is consistent with
it having no speed signal to hold xavier in the mix.

A round is one aggregation in both experiments, so this axis needs no
rescaling and carries no timing noise. It answers: *per unit of learning
actually merged, does mobility cost anything?*
