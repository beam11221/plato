Async federated learning · vehicle GPU tiers

Does the selector trade data quality for speed?

Two sweeps, 57 finished runs, one question: as the fleet's hardware gets more uneven, does client selection shift its preference from data quality (G_i) toward training speed (τ̂)? Every time axis below is the reconstructed simulated clock — tier sleep only, no host cost.

H-sweep 3 arms × 5 gaps × 2 seeds = 30 runs Fleet mix 3 arms × 4 fleets × 1 seed = 12 runs Target 55 % top-1, CIFAR-10 / ResNet-18

Before the figures

The time axis was rebuilt, not reported

PLATO advances its simulated clock to training_time + processing_time + comm_time. Only the first term is simulated; the other two are time.time() deltas for serialising and shipping a 42.69 MB payload — real work, on whichever GPU happened to run that cell, contended or not. Since these runs did not share a machine and some ran two lanes in parallel, the reported clock is not comparable across arms.

Every figure here therefore reads recompute_wall_time.py --floor 0: the recorded dispatch/merge schedule replayed with tier sleep as the only cost. The seed-1234/5678 runs had never been reconstructed before — that ran for this report.

host cost removed0.393 – 0.439 s per report
reported ÷ clean1.02 – 1.03× uniformly
reconstruction error0.02 – 0.29 % on 29 of 30
conclusions changednone — only the axis

The correction is small and nearly uniform, so it moves levels rather than orderings. That is the good outcome: it means the host contamination was never carrying a result, and now it demonstrably is not.

The mechanism is unambiguous. As the gap widens, gpu walks its xavier share from 49.3 % down to 36.3 % and lifts thor from 13.7 % to 20.7 % — monotone at every step. cpu moves the opposite way (48.8 → 52.8 % xavier): its belief cannot separate xavier from orin, since both clock 2.00 GHz, so it steers slightly toward the straggler. baseline sits flat near the pool.

The end-to-end timing follows, but with less room. gpu beats cpu at every gap (0.72–0.83×) and the margin widens with G. Against baseline it is 0.82–0.97× for G ≥ 10 and 1.12× at the control — the right sign everywhere, but the two-seed spread is ±0.02–0.16 h, so individual gaps are not separately significant.

Figure 4

The same runs, capacity divided out

Each arm ÷ that fleet's own baseline. This is the only view on this page that is about selection rather than hardware.

The advantage peaks in the middle, as pre-registered. gpu ÷ baseline runs 1.03 → 1.02 → 0.64 → 0.80. At 70/20/10 there are only ten thor clients and max_concurrency = 10 cannot keep picking them; at 15/35/50 half the pool is already fast, so preferring thor is nearly free. The middle is where a selector has something to do. This was the sweep's riskiest prediction and it held — though with n = 1 it is a single observation, not an interval.

Read before writing

What these figures do not support

The two topics are not the same experiment

They differ in two rendered keys, on purpose: t_round_model is "min" in the H-sweep and "queue" in the fleet-mix sweep, and target_accuracy is 0.6 vs 1.01. Everything else — dataset, model, sampler, concentration, seed mechanism, staleness bound, concurrency, anchor — is identical across all 72 configs. The G=30 / 50-35-15 cell exists in both sweeps and must not be pooled; it was deliberately re-run for topic 2.

The baseline is not a perfectly neutral control

Its dispatch lift drifts to 0.89 and 0.80 on xavier at the two fast-heavy fleets, against a pre-registered "~1.00 on every fleet". This looks like availability bias in random-skipping-busy — slow clients are occupied longer, so random sampling finds them less often — not a bug. But it means gpu's steering should be read against the baseline's lift, not against 1.00.

Topic 2 has n = 1

The fleet-mix sweep ran one seed, so Figures 3 and 4 carry no error bars and the 0.64 minimum has nothing separating it from run-to-run variance. Two more seeds would cost about the same as the H-sweep's second and third did.

Also worth carrying into the write-up: first-crossing times inherit roughly 10 % of evaluation noise, so any ratio inside about 1.15× is not resolvable from a single crossing. All curves here are smoothed with a 25-evaluation rolling median before the crossing is read.
